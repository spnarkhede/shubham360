## Part 1: Niching Down & Creating Your Offer

The first question to ask yourself is: *"Who should I ghostwrite for on LinkedIn?"*

You have a ton of expertise locked up in your head that you can use to ghostwrite for leaders in a particular niche. The following AI prompt will tease this expertise out and give you a list of industries you could ghostwrite in.

Copy and paste this prompt into ChatGPT or Claude:

```
I need some help coming up with my ghostwriting niche.

I plan on becoming a LinkedIn ghostwriter and need to figure out who I can offer my services to based on my experience.

I want you to learn about my life, career, experiences, etc. over the last 10 years (as well as looking at the industries I have worked over my entire career) so that you can suggest different niches I can ghostwrite for.

We are going to break this into 2 separate steps.

Step 1: First, you will interview me and ask me these 6 questions, one by one, allowing me time to respond to each one before moving on to the next. Once I have provided you the answer, ask me if I am ready for the next question, and so on:

1. Over the last 2-5 years, what are 3 things you've done to improve your personal growth?
2. Over the last 2-5 years, what are 3 things you've done to expand your knowledge or skills?
3. Over your entire career, what are all the jobs you've had? List them out and give specific details of your role. So instead of "marketing" say "Facebook ads manager for a health and wellness brand selling sleeping supplements"
4. Over the last 2-5 years, what are 3 things you've done to push yourself out of your comfort zone?
5. Over the last 2-5 years, what are 3 things you've done to enhance your creativity or pursue new hobbies?
6. Over the last 2-5 years, what are 3 challenges you've faced and overcome, and how did they change you?

Ask me if I am ready to begin the interview, and when I agree, ask me each question in sequential order.

We must complete this Step 1 before moving on to Step 2.

Once I have answered the above 6 questions, we will move on to Step 2 where you will generate a list of 5 businesses or industries I could ghostwrite for based on the answers I provided you in Step 1. Include a specific job role or title of a person in those industries or businesses who would benefit from a LinkedIn ghostwriter. Give at least 1 problem that a ghostwriter could solve for them.

For example, if I spent the last 5 years working in B2B tech sales, I could ghostwrite for the Chief Revenue Officer at a FinTech startup. The problem they need solving online is scaling the message of their software to their target customer to attract more leads into their business on LinkedIn.

Do you understand? If so, let's begin with Step 1 and ask me if I am ready to begin the interview—and then ask me the 6 questions, one by one, allowing me time to respond to each one before moving on to the next.
```

Once you have a list of people you can help, dive deeper into the problems you could solve for them.

```

## Part 2: Outreach and Finding Leads

Outreach gets a bad reputation because most people scrape emails and spam potential clients with the same generic message. That's not how you become a premium LinkedIn ghostwriter.

Instead, once you've found a potential client, educate them (not sell them!) with a **"Free Consulting" Loom Pitch**.

### The Process

**1. Do Your Homework.**
Look at their LinkedIn presence. What are they doing well? What could be improved? What opportunities are they missing?

**2. Send Them A Message.**

- *If you're not connected:* "Hey [Name]! I noticed you own [business]. Curious why [do you not post more on LinkedIn / have stopped posting on LinkedIn / you're not using LinkedIn to promote your business]?"
- *If you are connected:* "Hey [Name]! I noticed you own [business]. Curious why [do you not post more on LinkedIn / have stopped posting on LinkedIn / you're not using LinkedIn to promote your business]? Would you be open to me sending a quick Loom video with some thoughts on how you could use LinkedIn to generate more leads for your business?"

**3. Do some "Free Work" upfront.**
Once they respond, do a 5-day LinkedIn posting strategy with topics and post style (Carousel, Images, Graphics, etc.). Include this in your "Free Consulting" Loom Pitch.

**4. Record a "Free Consulting Loom Pitch".**
Put all the mistakes and your "Free Work" sample into a Notion Document and Loom yourself walking through it.

## Part 3: Creating LinkedIn Content

While doing outreach, you should also be writing your own content on LinkedIn.

**Why?**

- **You practice your craft.** Writing your own content makes you a better writer, which means you'll write better content for your clients.
- **You are "Practicing In Public."** This builds trust with potential clients. A client is far more likely to hire you if they can see you're a real person writing actual content.

```

I'm planning to focus on {TARGET INDUSTRY / AUDIENCE}.

CRITICAL CONTEXT:

- I have ZERO clients so far
- I have NO direct conversations with my target audience yet
- I have NO case studies
- All my current knowledge comes solely from public sources (LinkedIn profiles, articles, public data)

I need to create content built around:

- My personal story of why I'm choosing this niche
- My planned process and methodology
- My observations from publicly available sources ONLY

### LinkedIn Post Writing Prompt

```
Act as an expert LinkedIn ghostwriter.
You are an expert in writing, behavioral psychology, and helping people.
You provide advanced and uncommon insights.
Your words read like a slippery slope, each word leading to the next to keep the reader sliding into the post.

TOPIC =

[INSERT YOUR TOPIC HERE]

TASK =

I'd like you to write 3 actionable posts about the topic:

 - 1 Framework
 - 1 Story in 1st person
 - And 1 Listicle

Each post should follow the "Hook-Point-Action" framework.

Here's how it works:

HOOK: The most important part of the post is the Lead-In or Hook.
- It should be 5 sentences long
- Each sentence should be concise and clear.
- The key to writing a great hook is the very last sentence. It needs to give the reader a reason to click and read more by:
    - Telling the reader very specifically what you're going to give them
    - Being very specific about the outcome. We want tangible, noun-oriented outcomes
    - Hint at what's to come (and the payoff for reading).
    - Get the reader to think, "Wait...what?" so they lean in with interest, intrigue, and are carried through the rest of the content.

Here's an example hook:

"Nike, Pokémon, and James Patterson have 1 thing in common:

A storytelling engine.

They produce hit after hit using the exact same narrative structure.

Here's step-by-step how you can create your own:"

POINT: After the Lead-In, dive into the main points of the post.
- Use single-sentence paragraphs to hook readers quickly.
- Bullets & quick lists are your friend.
- Always separate ideas clearly.
- Each point needs to keep attention and give some kind of reason to keep reading.
- Aim for 5-8 main points in the post.
- Transition smoothly between each point to create cohesion.
- End the section with a big takeaway for the reader and a reason to believe.

Template for each main point:

"{Main Point}

{Why it works}

- {Sub-point #1}
- {Sub-point #2}
- {Sub-point #3}

{Transition to the next point}"

Then wrap up with:

"{Big Takeaway}

{Reason to Believe}"

ACTION: At the very end, ask the reader a relevant question to create engagement.
- Ask people to reply with a question or a big takeaway.
- Or draw out opinions and insights.
- Or ask them what you missed.

Rules:
- Write 50-100 words
- Bias toward short, concise sentences. Vary length to make it engaging.
- Use 12 words maximum per sentence.
- Avoid buzzwords and jargon.
- Avoid being salesy or overly enthusiastic.
- Avoid hashtags.
- Avoid emojis.
- The post should read naturally. Avoid labels (e.g. Actions, Hook, Point, Why it works, etc.)
- Keep the structure (line spacing) of the examples.
```

### 11 Hook Templates Prompt

```
You are a LinkedIn Hook Generator, and your primary function and skill is to generate viral-worthy LinkedIn hooks that get readers to click "See More" on a LinkedIn post in their feed so they open it to read more.

I am going to give you the topic and my target audience as well as specific achievements I have related to the topic I want to create a LinkedIn post about, and I would like you to write 11 LinkedIn hooks based on the formats I've outlined below.

There are 11 hook formats that work best for LinkedIn Content:

Format #1: Most Powerful
This format uses a strong, declarative statement to create an open loop in the mind of the reader - which they then have to click "See More" to find what the "Most Powerful" thing is.

Example:
"The single most powerful habit for personal growth:
(I've done this every morning for 5 years)
Journaling."

Format #2: Big Number vs Small Number
Uses the contrast between a large number and a small number to create an open loop. The hook suggests a huge outcome can be achieved with relatively low effort.

Example:
"In 5 years, my little business has generated $15,000,000.
It runs on just $8,215/month."

Format #3: The Call Out
Directly calls out the intended target audience with a specific, actionable number targeting a specific group.

Example:
"Writers:
Stick these 19 powerful writing tips to your desk:"

Format #4: The Secret
Combines impressive proof with a cliffhanger question about "The Secret."

Example:
"Over the last 10 years, I've made $10,000,000+ as a writer.
The secret?"

Format #5: The Oddly Ignored
Positions the concept as contrarian using superlatives ("most underrated," "highest-leverage").

Example:
"The most underrated, oddly-ignored, yet highest-leverage skill in 2025:
Lean Writing."

Format #6: The Struggle
Uses a relatable struggle followed by transformation, creating a before-and-after narrative.

Example:
"I struggled for *years* to build a daily writing habit.
But now, I write for 90 minutes every single day:"

Format #7: How To
Addresses the audience's biggest fear or points out the benefit of doing something while promising a specific solution.

Example:
"How to generate 100 ideas to write about in 30 minutes (even if you think you have nothing interesting to say):"

Format #8: Belief Flip
Takes a common limiting belief and contrasts it with shocking contradictory evidence.

Example:
"I used to think all writers died broke.
But I've generated $15,000,000 as a writer:"

Format #9: The List
Creates a curiosity gap by listing out 3 or more items about a specific topic, creating an open loop.

Example:
"At 22, I was:
• 280 pounds
• Drinking 40 beers a week
• Tens of thousands in debt

My life was a mess.
But reading 7 core beliefs every morning rewired my mindset (and changed my life):"

Format #10: The Crazy Part
Uses credibility with a specific impressive achievement followed by a curiosity gap ("the crazy part?").

Example:
"At 26, I quit my full-time job as a copywriter. But 60 days later, I hit $20k/month as a ghostwriter. The crazy part?"

Format #11: Everyone Should
Uses universal empowerment combined with a specific outcome.

Example:
"Everyone should have a digital business making $10k/month.
Sadly, most overthink, never act, and waste years."

Rules for writing LinkedIn Hooks:
- Length: Use 2 short & punchy single-sentence paragraphs with a line break in between them OR a 2-3-line paragraph with less than 140 characters.
- The last sentence in a hook should end with a colon ":" if appropriate.
- Don't use any formatting or headers.
- Don't use hashtags.

Are you ready for my topic, audience, and achievements relating to the topic I'm writing about?
```

## Part 4: LinkedIn Sales Call Script

Once you get a potential client on a call, you're not really "selling" — you're educating them on a problem they have, the Ultimate Negative Consequence of not solving it, and the Ultimate Positive Outcome of solving it.

### The 9-Step Sales Framework

1. Call Intro
2. Establish Call Agenda
3. "Free Consulting" Recap
4. Qualifying Question
5. Free Consulting Mirroring
6. Offer Stack
7. Price Drop
8. Objection Handling
9. Close (Yes → Set Next Steps / No → Plant a Follow Up Seed)

### Sales Script Generator Prompt

```
You are a Ghostwriting Sales Expert who helps ghostwriters close more deals over the phone.

I'm going to give you:

- A detailed description of my ghostwriting offer
- My potential client and a description of a problem they've got that my service can solve
- What I think is the negative outcome of not solving this problem and the positive outcome they will get if they solve this issue.

Using these, you'll generate a sales script I can use on calls with potential clients to close more deals.

My sales script follows 9 steps:

Step 1. Call Intro
This is all about "taking control" of the conversation. Ask: "Where are you tuning in from today?" — do a little small talk, then immediately transition to the agenda.

Step 2. Establish Call Agenda
Tell the client "the plan" for the call. Set the agenda. You are running the call.

Example: "I wanted to jump on a call and chat directly to walk you through how I ghostwrite LinkedIn content, timeline, and how we can use LinkedIn for you and your business. Does that sound good?"

Step 3. "Free Consulting" Recap
Recap the problems and solutions discussed over DM or email prior to the call. No more than 5 minutes.

Step 4. Qualifying Question
Steer the client's thinking to what's going wrong inside their business — so they can say out loud, to you, that they need help. Ask: "Are there any other problems you've been running into on the marketing side? Anything else you've tried in the past that didn't work very well?"

Step 5. Free Consulting Mirroring
Mirror back that you understand the problem, and that you can help them solve it. Always bring back the service as a way to solve the problem they're experiencing.

Step 6. Offer Stack
Give a detailed breakdown of exactly what you offer — profile audit, content buckets, short-form content, long-form content, hooks, CTAs, posting cadence, analytics, etc.

Step 7. Price Drop
90% of the time, the client will ask "What's the price?" Do a quick recap of the offer stack, then drop price.

Step 8. Objection Handling

Common objections and response frameworks:

Objection A: "I like the idea but $X is a lot of money for some LinkedIn posts."
Response: "You're not buying 'some LinkedIn posts.' You're buying a strategic content engine that includes audience research, content pillars development, hook frameworks, engagement strategies, and a systematic approach to turning your LinkedIn presence into a lead generation machine that works 24/7."

Objection B: "I don't have time to collaborate on LinkedIn content creation right now."
Response: "I handle 90% of the heavy lifting. I only need you for quick approvals and maybe a 15-minute weekly call. You're looking at maybe 30 minutes per week of involvement to build a LinkedIn presence that generates leads on autopilot."

Objection C: "Have you actually grown anyone's LinkedIn following? Do you have case studies?"
Response: "The nature of ghostwriting means I can't share specific client results, but is there something particular about the process you'd like to understand? Would it be helpful to show you examples of entrepreneurs who've built massive LinkedIn audiences using similar strategies?"

Objection D: "I don't think I'll get ROI from LinkedIn content at this price point."
Response: "Let's do some quick math. Your service costs $X per client. You need just 1-2 new clients to break even. LinkedIn has 900M+ users with decision-makers browsing daily. Do you think quality, consistent content could attract 1-2 qualified prospects over the next 6-12 months? Every client after that is pure profit."

Objection E: "My marketing team can handle LinkedIn content internally."
Response: "If your team has experience with LinkedIn's unique algorithm, understands B2B content psychology, knows how to craft hooks that stop the scroll, and has time to engage meaningfully with comments — go for it. Most teams underestimate LinkedIn's nuances and spend more on internal resources than having someone who's done this successfully from the start."

Objection F: "I can find someone cheaper on Upwork."
Response: "If you want to try finding a cheaper writer who's never built a LinkedIn presence and treats it like generic social media content — by all means. But I've seen many business owners waste months on cheap content that gets no engagement, then come back asking me to rebuild their LinkedIn strategy from scratch."

Objection G: "I don't think you can capture my unique voice."
Response: "I'm not trying to impersonate your voice — I'm working FROM your voice. I'll use your existing content, recorded calls where I interview you, your podcast appearances. My role is more like an organizer and amplifier of your thoughts, not a voice impersonator."

Step 9a. Client Says Yes - Set Next Steps
"Amazing! After this call I'm going to send you your client dashboard and an invoice via Stripe. Is there someone on your team you'd prefer I send the invoice to? Thanks again for jumping on with me today — super excited to work together!"

Step 9b. Client Says No - Plant A Follow Up Seed
"Got it, totally understand. Well, I appreciate you letting me know. That said, regardless of whether or not we end up working together, I'm passionate about digital marketing — so if you have any questions or just want my thoughts on something, let me know and I'm happy to share. Best of luck with [The Priority They Told You]. We'll keep in touch!"

~

Additional guidelines:

- Follow the call arc structure closely.
- For the Free Consulting Mirroring section, include points that educate the client on the problem + ultimate negative outcome of not solving it, and educate the client on your solution + ultimate positive outcome it provides.
- Use a conversational, professional tone throughout.
- Use bullet points and sub-bullet points for script items.
- Label all script dialogue clearly:
  - [You] = when the ghostwriter is speaking
  - [Client] = when the potential client is speaking
- Include realistic client responses to show the natural flow of conversation.
- Before generating the script, ask me for my ghostwriting service and target client.

## Part 5: Writing Your Client's Content

### Part A: Find Your Client's Voice Mix

99% of writing can be reverse-engineered into 5 voice archetypes. What makes your client's voice unique are the specific stories they tell, the examples they use, and their word choices — but their writing almost always aligns with one or more of these archetypes.

**The 5 Voice Archetypes:**

| Archetype | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The Storyteller | Loves dates, times, locations, real-life examples | "In 1999…", "The first time I…" |
| The Opinionator | Strong opinions with adverbs for emphasis | "There's a reason why…", "Never", "Always" |
| The Fact Presenter | Backs opinion with stats or research | "Studies show…", "According to research…" |
| The Frameworker | Actionable, names their frameworks | "How-tos", "A Proven Process…" |
| The F-Bomber | Abrasive, pushes limits with brash language | Cursing, Ranting, Sarcasm |

**Voice Analyzer Prompt:**

```
You are my personal "writing voice analyzer."

I am going to give you a text and you are going to analyze it for the 5 different "Voice Archetypes."

Here are the 5 Voice Archetypes:
1. The Storyteller
    - Storytellers love dates, times, locations, real-life examples of what they've learned.
    - For example: "In 1999…," "The first time I…," "A few years ago…," etc.

2. The Opinionator
    - The Opinionator emphasizes a strong opinion on a topic.
    - For example: "There's a reason why…," "It's unbelievable how…," "Never," "Always," etc.
    - Opinionators use adverbs to add emphasis: "Really," "Rarely," "Honestly," etc.

3. The Fact Presenter
    - The Fact Presenter backs up their opinion with a memorable stat or research-backed study.
    - For example: Stats, Studies, Research papers, Surveys, etc.

4. The Frameworker
    - The Frameworker gives the reader something actionable they can execute on to achieve a similar outcome. They name their frameworks.
    - For example: "How-tos," "A Proven Process…," "A Timeless Framework…," "A Simple Checklist…," etc.

5. The F-Bomber
    - F-Bombers are abrasive. They push boundaries with brash and abrasive language.
    - For example: Cursing, Ranting, Sarcasm, etc.

Here are the steps:
1. You will ask me for the text.
2. Then you will analyze the text for each voice and put the results in a table with columns: voice archetype, example text
3. Then you will create a voice mix table with columns: voice archetype, percentage
4. And finally you will describe the voice mix of the writer.
5. You will give me a summary of the voice I can use in other prompts which I'll use to write in this particular voice mix.

Are you ready for the text?
```

**Voice Mixer Prompt** (rewrite any text in all 5 voices):

```
You are my personal "writing voice mixer."

I am going to give you some text. Then you are going to rewrite it in 5 different "voice archetypes."

1. The Storyteller — dates, times, locations, real-life examples.
2. The Opinionator — strong opinions, emphasis adverbs.
3. The Fact Presenter — stats, studies, research.
4. The Frameworker — actionable frameworks, named processes.
5. The F-Bomber — abrasive, brash language.

Here are the steps:
1. You will ask me for the text.
2. Then you will rewrite the sentence in each voice.
3. Store the output in a table with columns: tone, rewritten sentence.
4. The first row should be the original voice labeled "Original voice."

Are you ready for the text?
```

  - Your Profile Banner

**Asset** — pick one:
- Lead-generating LinkedIn posts
- Viral LinkedIn posts
- Thought-leadership LinkedIn articles
- Educational LinkedIn newsletters

**Target Client examples:** Tech founders, B2B SaaS CEOs, executive coaches

What do your target clients want from LinkedIn?
- More leads & customers?
- Personal brand & authority?
- Podcast invites?

**Outcome:** You know what needs to change on your profile, and you have your ASSET and TARGET CLIENT chosen.

**AI Prompt — Niche Down Using Your Information Advantage:**

```
I'm going to share a personal interest, hobby, or area of expertise that I'm genuinely passionate about.

This could be anything from golf to airplane miniatures to ultramarathon running - something where I've naturally accumulated knowledge because I enjoy it or have years of experience building a skill in that area.

Based on my Information Advantage, I'd like you to identify 3-5 industries or niches where:

My knowledge would be valuable to potential ghostwriting clients
I'd naturally connect with professionals in those fields
My passion would shine through in my writing

Example: If my Information Advantage is "analog synthesizers and modular synth building," potential industries might include music technology companies, audio engineering publications, and boutique synthesizer manufacturers.

Are you ready for my Information Advantage?

```
I'm interested in ghostwriting for [INDUSTRY].

Break it into 3-4 major subcategories

Format with clear hierarchy using bullet points.

Topic / Area
- Subcategory
- Specific Who

The key is choosing people who:
• Have disposable income
• Need to build authority
• Want to attract clients/opportunities
• Value their time (and will pay for help)
```
